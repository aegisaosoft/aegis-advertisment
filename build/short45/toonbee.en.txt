FOUR-TWELVE — script for ToonBee (English)
MyEZToll · 14 scenes, one line each

Paste the fourteen lines below and nothing else — anything else on this page would
be read out by the narrator. Scene count 14, style Isometric, and Square 1:1 if the
cut is for the LinkedIn feed.

One line per scene on purpose: left to itself the generator merges whichever lines
it likes, and the one place that must not be merged is the price list in lines
10-12 — "no subscription, no set-up fee, nothing per vehicle" only works said one
at a time.

THE FOURTEEN LINES

Tuesday. Four-twelve in the afternoon. One of your cars goes through a toll. Nobody pays. Not yet.
The bill shows up three weeks later. With your name on it.
By then the renter is gone. Deposit refunded. Rental closed.
So you pay. And you call it the cost of doing business.
Same with the camera ticket. The fuel. The dent.
But the car knew. Its GPS was at that gantry, at that second.
And the booking knew who was driving. MyEZToll puts those two together and collects, from the renter's card.
One small fleet. One week. Four hundred twelve dollars — and it was going to be yours to swallow.
So what does the platform cost you? Nothing.
No subscription. No set-up fee. No charge per vehicle.
Connecting the GPS you already run is free as well.
The only paid option is our own telematics hardware.
You pay nothing. And you get a share of everything that comes back.
Stop losing. Start earning.


WHAT EACH SCENE SHOWS — optional, if the generator asks

1. Late afternoon. A rental car passes under a toll gantry; the sensor blinks.
2. A calendar turning three weeks; an envelope landing on the owner's desk, his name on it.
3. The renter driving away, the deposit handed back, the rental folder closing.
4. The owner at his desk, paying a bill he did not incur.
5. More arriving: a camera ticket, a fuel receipt, a dented panel.
6. The same crossing replayed — a GPS pin dropping on the car at the gantry, with a timestamp.
7. A booking record lighting up with the driver's name; the two facts snap together and the charge turns around onto the renter's card.
8. One number, large: $412.68. One small fleet, one week.
9. The price-tag question, asked plainly.
10. Three empty price tags: no subscription, no set-up fee, nothing per vehicle.
11. A GPS unit he already owns, plugging in for free.
12. Beside it, one small paid box: the telematics hardware, clearly the exception.
13. Money flowing toward the owner instead of away from him.
14. End card, still: the MyEZToll wordmark and "Stop losing. Start earning."


THINGS THAT MUST STAY TRUE

- $412.68 over 39 crossings is one real week of one real small fleet.
- Free means free for the fleet owner: no subscription, no set-up fee, no charge
  per vehicle, and connecting an existing GPS costs nothing either. Charges are
  collected from the renter who incurred them, and the owner receives a share of
  everything recovered.
- The one exception is our own telematics hardware, an optional paid module. It is
  named out loud in line twelve and must stay named — a "free" claim with the
  exception quietly removed is the one thing here that would be a lie.
- "Four-twelve" in line one and "four hundred twelve" in line eight are the same
  number returning as a bill. Keep both or neither.
- This is a business owner losing money, not a children's story: isometric or
  another flat, adult style, never storybook or Pixar-like.
